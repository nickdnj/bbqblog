Our Equipment

Meet our grills: the Kay Park SF163.5

These aren't backyard hardware-store grills — they're the same heavy-gauge, commercial park grills you'll find in state and national parks, built to be lit thousands of times by thousands of hands. Here's exactly what we put in the grove.

SF16 flip-up-grate park grill · 3.5 = upgraded 3½″ heavy-duty pedestal

The whole firebox is cut and welded from 3/16″ steel — a continuous weld with no seams to crack, a reinforced ash lip to keep embers contained, rounded corners so nobody catches a sharp edge, and drain holes so a Jersey Shore downpour runs right out instead of pooling into a soggy mess.

The cooking grate is no flimsy wire rack: ½″ steel rods on 1-inch centers, so it holds heat, won't sag under a loaded foil pan, and leaves those proper thick sear marks. It flips back on a hinge — that's the trick to public grilling. Flip it up to dump in charcoal and light it, flip it up again to rake out cold ash, no lifting a hot grate off and hunting for somewhere to set it down. The handles are non-removable cool-spring handles, the public-park standard, so they stay put and stay cool enough to touch.

Best feature for our windy waterfront: the whole grill rotates a full 360° on its pedestal. Wind kicking up off the river? Just spin the firebox so your back blocks the gust — your fire lights faster and burns steadier. The grill head sits on a 3½″ galvanized steel pedestal set in concrete (the ".5" in our model — the upgrade over the standard 2-3/8″ post), and every one has a bolt-on utility shelf for landing your platter, tongs, and a cold drink.

11 grills · ~1,078 lbs of American steel · built by Kay Park Recreation in Janesville, Iowa — "Making People Places, People Friendly Since 1954."

Start Here

Your first cookout, in 6 simple steps

Charcoal isn't hard — it's just different from the gas grill at home. Follow these six steps and you'll be the one showing the next neighbor how it's done.

🔥

Gas vs. charcoal, in one sentence: instead of turning a knob, you light a pile of charcoal, wait about 15–20 minutes for it to glow and turn ashy-gray, then cook over that heat. That wait is the only real difference — and it's the best part.

1

Plan & reserve

The grills are first-come for small groups. Hosting 12 or more? Reserve ahead so a spot is waiting for you, and skim the picnic rules once before you go.

Tip: pick a spot with the wind at your back — it makes lighting easier.

2

Pack the essentials

The grills, grates, and ash bins are here for you. You bring the rest:

  • A bag of charcoal briquettes
  • A chimney starter or approved lighter fluid
  • A long lighter or matches
  • Long-handled tongs & a spatula
  • Cooler with ice for raw food
  • Foil, paper towels, and trash bags
3

Light the coals

Easiest way: fill a chimney starter with charcoal, stuff newspaper underneath, and light it. In ~15 minutes the coals are ready — no fluid, no guesswork.

Prefer lighter fluid? Squirt it on an unlit pile, wait a minute, then light — and never add more once it's lit.

4

Wait for the gray glow

Coals are ready when they're glowing and covered in gray ash — not when flames are shooting up. Spread them out, and slide most to one side so you have a cooler "safe zone" to move food to.

No thermometer? Hold your hand 5 inches above the grate — pull back at "3 Mississippi" and it's hot enough.

5

Cook with confidence

Start with forgiving foods — burgers, hot dogs, chicken, veggies. Lay them over the coals, flip once, and use a $12 meat thermometer to be sure:

  • Chicken & turkey: 165°F
  • Burgers & ground meat: 160°F
  • Steaks, chops, fish: 145°F
6

Cool down & clean up

Let the coals burn all the way down and go cold — this can take a couple of hours. Only then, scoop the cold ash into the metal ash bins. Brush the grate, bag your trash, and leave it better than you found it for the next neighbor.

Policies & Safety

Picnic & grill rules everyone must follow

Based on Monmouth County Parks regulations, Borough of Monmouth Beach ordinances, NFPA guidance, and Wharfside HOA policies.

General conduct & reservations
  • Reserve your grill slot in advance for groups over 12; gatherings of 50+ require Board approval 7 days prior.
  • Arrive on time and limit sessions to 2 hours when others are waiting.
  • No alcohol, controlled substances, glass bottles, or fireworks in the picnic area.
  • Keep music to conversational levels; amplification needs HOA sign-off.
Grill use & fire safety
  • Grills stay on their pedestals—do not relocate or modify hardware.
  • Maintain a 10 ft safety perimeter from buildings, railings, and landscaping.
  • Use chimney starters, approved lighter fluid, or instant-light briquettes only.
  • Keep a sand bucket, spray bottle, or extinguisher within reach; never leave coals unattended.
Food safety & hygiene
  • Transport perishables on ice and keep raw and cooked items separate.
  • Follow USDA temperatures: 165°F poultry, 160°F ground meats, 145°F whole cuts (3-minute rest), 140–145°F seafood.
  • Use clean utensils and cutting boards; wash hands or use sanitizer frequently.
  • Leftovers must be chilled within 2 hours (1 hour if above 90°F ambient).
Cleanup & ash disposal
  • Let coals burn down and cool fully. Scoop cold ash into the covered metal bins provided.
  • Brush grates, wipe shelves, and remove all trash, decorations, and food scraps.
  • Oil grates lightly after cleaning to prevent rust in the coastal climate.
  • Report damage, loose hardware, or corrosion via the HOA maintenance request.
Pool, beach & pet etiquette
  • Follow lifeguard directions; shower before using pool; no diving in shallow zones.
  • Pets must remain leashed, cleaned up after, and off beach areas March 15 – Oct 1.
  • Keep smoke and music away from neighboring groups and residences.
  • Respect dunes and landscaping—no digging, staking, or attaching décor.
Emergency plan
  • Dial 911 for emergencies, then notify the HOA office at 732-245-4057.
  • Fire extinguishers and first-aid kits are stored in the pool house lobby.
  • Know the location of ash bins, sand buckets, and hose bibs before lighting.
  • Complete an incident report within 24 hours for any injury or equipment issue.

Wharfside Waterfront Showcase feast plan

Finalize guest list, confirm reservations, marinate proteins, par-bake desserts, and print station signage.
